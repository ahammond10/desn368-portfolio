What design choices did you make and why?

I wanted the page to feel like Pokémon Emerald, so I made the background dark green and the sections a lighter green. I picked yellow for the headings because it reminded me of the Pokémon logo.

I used a pixel font for the headings because it goes with the sprites. For the directions, I used Roboto because it’s easier to read a full paragraph in that font.

What was difficult to code? How did you solve it?

One issue I noticed was the tip at the bottom of the shiny Mew section. The sentence was all on one line, and I had to scroll sideways inside the box to read it.

I split the tip into three short lines in the HTML. The pre tag keeps the line breaks where I put them, so I could decide where the sentence broke and keep the tip box from my design.

How did you adapt your design for mobile?

On desktop, the sidebar takes up 300px. That would take up most of a phone screen, so at 640px and below I moved the links above the guide and let them wrap onto another line.

I hid the sprites on mobile so each checklist could use the full width. I also stacked the two round footer links so you can reach both of them by scrolling down.

What did you learn about Flexbox or CSS positioning?

I learned that putting display: flex on the main container puts the sidebar and guide next to each other. For mobile, I could switch that same container to a column and keep the sections in order.

I also learned why the sticky sidebar uses align-self: flex-start. That keeps the sidebar the height of its links and gives it room to stay in view while I scroll through the longer guide.

What would you add or change with more time?

I’d add a map of the warp panels in Team Aqua’s Hideout. My Master Ball section tells you to look for the room with four item balls, but a map could show which panels actually take you there.

I’d also put a regular pink Mew next to the shiny blue Mew in the last section. Someone doing the hunt for the first time could see exactly which one they’re looking for.
